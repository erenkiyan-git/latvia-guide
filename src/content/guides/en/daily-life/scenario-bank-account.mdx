<QuickAnswer>
Do not try to open a traditional Latvian bank account before you have your physical Residence Permit eID card. Use a digital bank like Revolut or Wise for your first few months.
</QuickAnswer>

## The Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Rules

Latvian banks (Swedbank, SEB, Citadele) are subject to incredibly strict European Anti-Money Laundering regulations. They view non-resident foreigners as high-risk clients.

If you walk into a traditional bank with just your home country's passport and say "I want to open an account":
1. They will ask you to prove your "strong connection to Latvia" (e.g., an employment contract).
2. They will charge you a massive **"Document Review Fee"** (often between 150 and 300 EUR).
3. **You must pay this fee upfront, and it is non-refundable.**
4. They will review your application for 2 to 4 weeks. They frequently reject applications, and you will lose the 300 EUR fee.

## The Solution

**Wait for your Permit.**
Once PMLP approves your visa and issues you a Latvian Residence Permit (eID card) with a Personal Code (Personas kods), you are officially a resident. 
With this card, traditional banks will usually open an account for you immediately, and the massive non-resident review fees are waived.

<FAQ title="How do I get paid while I wait?">
If your employer needs to pay your salary before your traditional bank account is open, use **Revolut** or **Wise**. Most modern Latvian employers will happily pay your salary into a Revolut or Wise IBAN (especially if it is a European IBAN starting with LT or BE). These apps are free to open and do not require Latvian residency.
</FAQ>

<WarningBox>
**The Utility Bill Catch:** Some older landlords or building managers might demand you pay your rent from a local Latvian bank account (starting with LV). You can explain that a SEPA transfer from Revolut is legally exactly the same, but it's best to open a local Swedbank or SEB account as soon as your Residence Permit arrives to make daily life easier.
</WarningBox>
