<QuickAnswer>
Jāņi (Midsummer) on June 23/24 is the biggest and most important celebration of the year. During national holidays, all state institutions and banks are closed, and public transport runs on a holiday schedule.
</QuickAnswer>

## The Major Holidays

When these days occur, you get a paid day off work. If a major holiday falls on a weekend, the following Monday is usually given as a day off.

### Jāņi and Līgo (June 23 & 24)
This is the Latvian Midsummer. It is a massive pagan celebration of the longest day of the year. 
* **What happens:** The entire city of Riga empties out. Everyone goes to the countryside to jump over bonfires, wear oak-leaf and flower crowns, drink massive amounts of beer, and eat caraway cheese (Jāņu siers). Do not plan any serious business during this week.

### Independence Day (November 18)
The proclamation of the Republic of Latvia in 1918. 
* **What happens:** A very patriotic and solemn holiday. There is usually a military parade by the Daugava river in Riga, a speech by the President, and a massive torchlight procession through the city center in the evening.

### Lāčplēsis Day (November 11)
Not a full public holiday (people still work), but a highly significant memorial day for the soldiers who fought for Latvia's independence.
* **What happens:** Thousands of people gather at the Riga Castle wall and along the river to light candles in the dark. It is a beautiful, quiet spectacle.

### Christmas (Ziemassvētki)
Latvians celebrate Christmas on the evening of December 24th (Christmas Eve). December 25th and 26th are official holidays. 
* **Traditions:** Dragging a Yule log, eating grey peas with bacon (to leave the old year's tears behind), and singing.

## Other Official Days Off
* **New Year's Day:** January 1
* **Easter (Lieldienas):** Good Friday and Easter Monday.
* **Labour Day:** May 1
* **Restoration of Independence Day:** May 4 (Commemorating the break from the Soviet Union in 1990).
