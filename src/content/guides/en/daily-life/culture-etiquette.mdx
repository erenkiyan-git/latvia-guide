<QuickAnswer>
Latvians are reserved, polite, and value personal space. Do not mistake their lack of small talk for rudeness. If invited to someone's home, always bring a gift, take off your shoes, and bring an odd number of flowers.
</QuickAnswer>

## Social Interactions

* **Personal Space:** Latvians value their physical distance. Do not stand too close to people in queues, and avoid physical contact (like hugging or touching arms) with people you do not know very well.
* **Small Talk:** "How are you?" is not a greeting in Latvia; it is a literal question. If you ask a Latvian how they are, expect an honest answer about their bad back or stressful job. Do not force small talk on cashiers or people on the bus.
* **Smiling:** Latvians do not smile at strangers on the street. A smile is reserved for friends and family. Smiling constantly at strangers is viewed as suspicious or insincere.

## Visiting a Latvian Home

If you are invited to a Latvian's apartment for a dinner or party, there are strict rules:
1. **Punctuality:** Arrive exactly on time. 
2. **Shoes:** You MUST take off your shoes immediately inside the front door. The host will usually offer you a pair of guest slippers (čības).
3. **The Gift:** Never arrive empty-handed. Bring a bottle of wine, some high-quality chocolate (like Laima), or a nice pastry.
4. **Flowers:** If you bring flowers, you must bring an **odd number** (1, 3, 5). Even numbers (2, 4, 6) are strictly reserved for funerals and cemeteries. 

## The Language

Latvian is the sole official language. It is a Baltic language, completely unrelated to Russian or Germanic languages (except for Lithuanian). 
* **The Russian Language:** Due to the Soviet occupation, a large portion of the population speaks Russian. However, the linguistic and political situation is highly sensitive. As a foreigner, it is always better to start a conversation in English rather than assuming the person speaks or wants to speak Russian.
* **Try to speak Latvian:** Even learning to say "Sveiki" (Hello) and "Paldies" (Thank you) will earn you immense respect from the locals.

<FAQ title="Are Latvians really that introverted?">
Yes, until you become their friend. The cultural "coconut" analogy applies perfectly to Latvians: they have a hard, reserved outer shell, but once you break through it, they are incredibly warm, loyal, and hospitable inside.
</FAQ>
