<QuickAnswer>
Latvian winters are cold, snowy, and extremely dark. To survive comfortably, you need proper waterproof boots, a windproof winter coat, Vitamin D supplements, and a light reflector for your coat.
</QuickAnswer>

## 1. The Darkness
The cold is manageable; the darkness is what shocks most foreigners. In December, the sun rises around 09:00 and sets before 16:00. The sky is often grey and overcast for weeks.
* **Vitamin D:** Start taking Vitamin D supplements in October. Almost everyone in Latvia has a Vitamin D deficiency in winter. It massively helps with energy levels and fighting off seasonal depression.
* **Reflectors (Atstarotājs):** By law, if you are walking outside city limits or in poorly lit areas at night, you must wear a reflector on your clothing. Even in Riga, it is highly recommended so drivers can see you in the rain and snow. You can buy them in any supermarket.

## 2. Clothing

Do not buy "fashion" winter coats. You need function.
* **The Coat:** You need a thick, windproof, and waterproof parka. The wind coming off the Baltic sea makes -5°C feel like -15°C. 
* **The Shoes:** This is the most important item. The streets of Riga in winter are often covered in a mix of snow, salt, and slush (slapjdraņķis). If your shoes are not waterproof, your feet will get wet, and you will freeze. Buy high-quality, waterproof winter boots with thick rubber soles.
* **Layers:** Latvians dress in layers. Apartments are usually heated very well (sometimes too well). You want a warm coat for outside, but just a light sweater for inside the office.

## 3. Black Ice (Melnais ledus)
When temperatures fluctuate around 0°C, the wet pavements freeze into invisible "black ice". Walk carefully, like a penguin, taking short, flat steps. Do not walk with your hands in your pockets; you need them to break your fall if you slip.

<WarningBox>
**Falling Icicles:** In the Old Town and Center, be very careful walking directly under the roofs of old buildings. When the snow melts, massive icicles (lāstekas) fall to the pavement and can cause severe injuries. 
</WarningBox>
