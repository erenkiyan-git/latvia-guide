<QuickAnswer>
Latvia has a strict zero-tolerance policy for corruption. Attempting to bribe a traffic police officer or a government official will result in immediate arrest and criminal charges.
</QuickAnswer>

## A Different Era

If you hear stories about people easily bribing traffic cops in Eastern Europe, those stories are usually 20 years old. Latvia has aggressively reformed its institutions.

Today, state bodies like KNAB (The Corruption Prevention and Combating Bureau) are extremely powerful. While high-level political corruption scandals occasionally make the news, **everyday street-level corruption is essentially non-existent.**

## Interacting with the Police

If you are pulled over for speeding:
* The police officer will be wearing a body camera.
* Their police car is equipped with audio and video recording equipment.
* If you slip a 50 EUR note into your driving licence and hand it to the officer, **you will be arrested for bribery (Kukuļdošana)**. You will be placed in handcuffs, your car will be impounded, and you will face criminal prosecution.

Just accept the speeding ticket. You pay it officially via bank transfer later.

## Bureaucracy (PMLP, Hospitals, Universities)

Do not attempt to bribe officials to speed up your visa process.
Do not attempt to bribe university professors to pass an exam.
Do not attempt to bribe doctors in state hospitals to jump the queue. 

<WarningBox>
**"Gifts" vs. Bribes:** In the past, it was common to bring a box of chocolates or coffee to a doctor to say "thank you" after a successful treatment. However, even this practice is now heavily discouraged by ethics guidelines. It is much safer to simply say a sincere "Paldies" (Thank you).
</WarningBox>
