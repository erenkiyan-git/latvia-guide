<QuickAnswer>
In Latvia, you cannot buy medicine (like Aspirin or Ibuprofen) in a supermarket; you must go to a pharmacy (Aptieka). Most antibiotics and strong medications require a doctor's digital prescription.
</QuickAnswer>

## Finding a Pharmacy (Aptieka)

Pharmacies in Latvia are marked by a green cross. You will find them in every shopping mall, on major streets, and next to clinics. 
* **Major chains:** Mēness aptieka, Benu aptieka, Euroaptieka, and Apotheka.
* **Over-the-counter (OTC):** Basic painkillers (Ibuprofen, Paracetamol), cold medicines, vitamins, and allergy pills are kept behind the counter. You must ask the pharmacist for them.
* **Language:** Most pharmacists speak English and Russian and can offer basic medical advice if you have a mild cold or stomach ache.

## E-Prescriptions (E-Recepte)

Latvia has a fully digital prescription system.
If you visit a doctor (or have a remote consultation) and they prescribe you medication, they do not give you a piece of paper. Instead, they enter the prescription into the national "E-veselība" system.

**How to pick up your medicine:**
1. Walk into any pharmacy.
2. Hand the pharmacist your Latvian ID card or Passport.
3. They scan your ID, see the doctor's prescription on their computer, and give you the medicine.

<WarningBox>
**Bringing medicine from home:** If you rely on specific prescription medication (like ADHD stimulants or strong painkillers), do not assume you can easily get them in Latvia. Bring a supply from home, accompanied by an official doctor's note translated into English, to prove they are for personal use at the border.
</WarningBox>

## 24-Hour Pharmacies (Dežūraptieka)

Standard pharmacies close around 20:00 or 21:00. If you need medicine in the middle of the night, you must find a *Dežūraptieka* (Duty Pharmacy). There are usually only a few open 24/7 in Riga (often located in the city center or attached to major hospitals). Use Google Maps and search for "24h aptieka".
