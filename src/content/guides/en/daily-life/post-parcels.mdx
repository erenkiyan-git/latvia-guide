<QuickAnswer>
Latvians rarely use traditional home delivery for packages. Almost all e-commerce and personal packages are delivered to automated parcel lockers (Omniva or DPD) located outside supermarkets.
</QuickAnswer>

## The Parcel Locker System (Pakomāts)

If you order something online from a Latvian or Baltic store, you will usually be asked to select a "Pakomāts" (Parcel machine) instead of entering your home address.

* **The Big Players:** Omniva, DPD, and Smartpost.
* **How it works:** 
  1. You select the locker closest to your home (e.g., outside your local Rimi).
  2. When the package arrives, you receive an SMS with a 6-digit door code.
  3. You go to the machine, punch in the code on the touch screen, and a door pops open with your package.
* **Why it's great:** It is incredibly cheap, fast, and you can pick up your package 24/7 without waiting at home for a courier. 

## The National Post (Latvijas Pasts)

You will use *Latvijas Pasts* (Latvian Post) for receiving letters from the government, sending postcards, or receiving international packages from outside the Baltics.

* **Receiving International Packages:** If your grandmother sends you a box from the USA or India, it will go to the main post office assigned to your address. You will get a small paper slip in your home mailbox (or an SMS) telling you to go to the post office with your ID to collect it.

## Customs and Import Taxes (Muita)

If you order clothes from the UK, electronics from China (AliExpress), or receive a gift from outside the EU, **you must pay import taxes.**

<WarningBox>
**Do not ignore the customs SMS.** When a package from outside the EU arrives in Latvia, Latvijas Pasts will send you a link to the national customs declaration system (EDS). You must fill out an electronic declaration and pay the 21% VAT (and customs duties if the value is high) online. If you do not do this within a few weeks, the package will be returned to the sender.
</WarningBox>
