<QuickAnswer>
Most apartments in Riga use city central heating. It is turned on automatically in October and off in April. You cannot control the dates, and your utility bill will increase significantly during these months.
</QuickAnswer>

## City Central Heating (Rīgas Siltums)

The majority of apartment buildings in Riga (especially Soviet-era blocks like in Purvciems or Imanta) are connected to the city's central heating grid.

* **How it works:** Hot water is pumped from the city's power plants directly into the radiators in your apartment.
* **When does it start?** The building management company turns the heating on when the outdoor temperature drops below a certain average (usually in mid-October) and turns it off in spring (April/May).
* **Control:** In older buildings, you cannot control the temperature in your room. If it gets too hot, people just open a window. In newer or renovated buildings, you have adjustable thermostats on the radiators.

## The Cost of Winter

Because heating is expensive, your utility bill (komunālie maksājumi) will look very different depending on the season.
* **Summer Bill (June - August):** Might be 50 - 80 EUR (mostly water and maintenance).
* **Winter Bill (December - February):** Can easily jump to 150 - 300 EUR, depending on the size of the apartment and the insulation of the building.

## Other Heating Types

If an apartment is not connected to central heating, it will have:
* **Gas Heating (Gāzes apkure):** The apartment has its own gas boiler. You control the temperature, but gas prices can fluctuate heavily.
* **Electric Heating (Elektriskā apkure):** Usually found in small or very old apartments. **Avoid this if possible.** Heating a Latvian apartment with electricity in January is incredibly expensive.
* **Wood Stove (Malkas apkure):** Found in older wooden houses (e.g., in Āgenskalns). It is very cheap, but you must manually buy, store, and burn wood in the stove every single day. 

<FAQ title="How do I keep my bills low?">
The best way is to rent an apartment in a "Renovated building" (Renovēta ēka) or a "New project" (Jaunais projekts). These buildings have modern insulation, so less heat escapes through the walls, making the winter heating bill much cheaper than in an unrenovated Soviet block.
</FAQ>
