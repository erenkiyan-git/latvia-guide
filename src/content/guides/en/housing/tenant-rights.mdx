<QuickAnswer>
Latvian rental laws provide decent protection for tenants. A landlord cannot evict you without a court order, cannot enter the apartment without your permission, and cannot raise the rent arbitrarily during a fixed-term contract.
</QuickAnswer>

## The New Residential Tenancy Law
In 2021, Latvia introduced a new "Residential Tenancy Law" (Dzīvojamo telpu īres likums). The new law is stricter and requires all new rental contracts to be for a fixed term (no more open-ended contracts lasting forever).

## Landlord Rights and Restrictions
* **Entering the apartment:** The landlord cannot just use their key and walk in whenever they want. They must give you notice and agree on a time to inspect the property, usually limited to once a year unless there is an emergency (like a burst pipe).
* **Raising the rent:** During a fixed-term contract, the landlord cannot increase the cold rent unless the contract explicitly includes a clause stating how and when the rent can be indexed (e.g., tied to official inflation rates).
* **Eviction:** If you stop paying rent, the landlord cannot just throw your bags onto the street and change the locks. They must give you official warnings and, ultimately, take you to court to get a legal eviction order. (However, the 2021 law made this court process much faster than before).

## Tenant Responsibilities
* **Paying on time:** You must pay the rent and utilities exactly on the dates specified in the contract.
* **Minor repairs:** Usually, the tenant is responsible for minor maintenance (changing lightbulbs, unblocking the sink). Major repairs (a broken boiler, leaking roof) are the landlord's responsibility.
* **Subletting:** You cannot sublet the apartment on Airbnb or to other students unless the landlord gives written permission.

## Dispute Resolution
If you have a serious dispute with your landlord (e.g., they refuse to return your deposit despite no damage), you can seek help from the Consumer Rights Protection Centre (PTAC) or file a claim in the civil court.
