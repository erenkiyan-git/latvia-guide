<QuickAnswer>
A rental contract must be in writing. Make sure it explicitly allows you to declare your address (deklarēšanās), states the exact notice period for moving out, and clearly defines who pays for which utilities.
</QuickAnswer>

## Essential Clauses to Check

Before you sign an *Īres līgums* (Rental Contract), ensure it covers these points:

### 1. Address Declaration (Deklarēšanās)
As a foreigner, you legally must declare your address. 
* **Check:** Does the contract allow this? Some landlords operate illegally (to avoid paying the 10% rental income tax) and will forbid you from declaring the address. Do not sign with these landlords. 

### 2. Duration and Termination (Uzteikums)
* **Check:** Is it a fixed-term contract (e.g., exactly 12 months) or open-ended?
* **Notice Period:** If you want to move out early, how much notice must you give? The standard is 1 month. Make sure the landlord does not lock you into paying the entire year's rent if you leave early.

### 3. Rent and Utilities
* **Check:** Does the listed rent include anything, or is it "cold rent"? Make sure the contract specifies that you pay rent PLUS utilities (Komunālie maksājumi), and ensure it lists exactly which utilities you cover (heating, water, electricity, internet, building management fee).

### 4. The Security Deposit (Drošības nauda)
* **Check:** The standard deposit is 1 or 2 months' rent. The contract must state exactly under what conditions the deposit will be withheld (e.g., unpaid bills, damage beyond normal wear and tear) and how many days after you move out it will be returned.

## Language of the Contract

Contracts are usually drafted in Latvian. If you do not understand Latvian, you should ask for a bilingual contract (Latvian/English). If the landlord refuses, use a translation app or ask a local friend to read it. **Never sign a document you do not understand.**

<FAQ title="Does the contract need to be notarized?">
No, a standard rental contract does not need to be notarized to be legally binding. However, some landlords prefer to register the contract in the Land Register (Zemesgrāmata) for extra legal protection.
</FAQ>
