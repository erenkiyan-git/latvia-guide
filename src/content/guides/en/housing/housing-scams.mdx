<QuickAnswer>
The golden rule of renting in Latvia: Never transfer a deposit or the first month's rent before you have physically viewed the apartment, met the landlord (or their official agent), and signed a written contract.
</QuickAnswer>

## Common Rental Scams

The rental market in Riga, especially around August and September when international students arrive, is full of scammers. They usually operate on Facebook groups.

### 1. The "I am out of the country" scam
**How it works:** A "landlord" posts pictures of a beautiful, cheap apartment in the Old Town. When you message them, they say they currently live in Spain/UK/Germany and cannot show you the flat in person. They promise to mail you the keys via DHL if you wire them a 500 EUR deposit via Western Union or to a foreign bank account.
**The Truth:** The apartment does not exist (or they stole the photos from Airbnb). Once you send the money, they block you.

### 2. The "Fake Agent"
**How it works:** Someone claims to be a real estate agent. They show you a real apartment (which they rented on Airbnb for one night). You sign a fake contract and give them the deposit in cash. The next day, you discover the real owner knows nothing about it, and your "agent" has vanished.
**The Truth:** Always ask to see the agent's ID and verify the ownership of the apartment on the public Land Register (Zemesgrāmata) if you have doubts.

### 3. "Pay to View"
**How it works:** The landlord says the apartment is very popular and demands a 50 EUR "viewing fee" or "reservation fee" just to schedule a time to see it.
**The Truth:** Legitimate landlords never charge a fee to view an apartment.

## Red Flags Checklist
Walk away immediately if:
* The price is suspiciously low for the location and quality.
* The landlord refuses to show you the apartment in person (or via live video call with a trusted local friend).
* They pressure you to pay a deposit immediately "before someone else takes it."
* The bank account name for the deposit does not match the name of the landlord on the contract.

<WarningBox>
Even if you are desperate for an apartment before you arrive, it is much safer to book an Airbnb or a hostel for your first two weeks in Riga and do your apartment hunting in person.
</WarningBox>
