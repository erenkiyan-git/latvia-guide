<QuickAnswer>
The price listed on a rental ad is usually just the "cold rent" (īre). You must also budget for "utilities" (komunālie maksājumi), which include building maintenance, water, and heating, and can be very expensive in winter.
</QuickAnswer>

## The "Cold Rent" (Īre)

When you look at an ad on SS.com that says "350 EUR/month", this is almost always the cold rent. This money goes directly to the landlord for the right to live in the space.

## The Utilities (Komunālie maksājumi)

Utilities are billed separately, usually by the building management company (apsaimniekotājs). You pay this bill every month. It typically includes:
* **Water and Sewage:** Usually metered. You submit your water meter readings (ūdens skaitītāji) at the end of every month.
* **Waste Disposal:** A fixed monthly fee.
* **Building Maintenance (Apsaimniekošanas maksa):** A fee paid to the management company to clean the stairwells, fix the roof, and maintain the yard.
* **Heating:** This is the big one. (Read more in the [Winter Heating Guide](./winter-heating-bills)).

*Note: Electricity and Internet are usually separate contracts that you set up yourself or pay directly based on the meter.*

## The Security Deposit (Drošības nauda)

When you sign the contract, you must pay the first month's rent PLUS a security deposit.
* **Standard amount:** Equal to 1 or 2 months of cold rent. 
* **Purpose:** To protect the landlord if you damage the furniture, break the washing machine, or move out without paying your last utility bill.
* **Return:** The landlord must return the deposit after you move out and they verify no damage was done. 

<WarningBox>
When viewing an apartment, always ask the landlord to show you the utility bills from the previous **January** and **July**. This is the only way to know the true cost of living there. A "cheap" 250 EUR apartment might have 200 EUR utility bills in winter due to bad insulation.
</WarningBox>
