<QuickAnswer>
If you enter Latvia visa-free or on a short-stay Schengen visa, you can only stay for **90 days within any 180-day period**. The 180-day window is a "rolling" window that moves every single day.
</QuickAnswer>

## How the Rolling Window Works

The rule is often misunderstood. It is not 90 days per calendar year. 

To check if you are allowed to be in Latvia (or anywhere in the Schengen area) today, border guards will look backwards exactly 180 days from today. If they count the days you were inside the Schengen area during that 180-day period, the total must not exceed 90. 

Because the window looks backwards from *today*, the 180-day period shifts forward every single day. 

### Example

* You spend 90 continuous days in Latvia. 
* You must now leave the Schengen area entirely. 
* You cannot return the next day. You must wait 90 days outside the Schengen area before you have built up enough "allowance" to re-enter.

## What Counts as "Inside Schengen"?

* **Arrival and Departure Days:** The day you enter the Schengen area counts as Day 1. The day you leave the Schengen area counts as a full day, even if your flight is at 00:05 AM.
* **Transit:** Transiting through another Schengen airport (e.g., flying Istanbul -> Frankfurt -> Riga) counts from the moment you pass passport control in Frankfurt.
* **Other Countries:** The 90 days is a combined total for the *entire* Schengen zone. If you spend 30 days in Germany and 60 days in Latvia, you have used your 90 days. 

<FAQ title="Does time spent on a Residence Permit count?">
No. If you hold a valid Latvian long-stay visa (Type D) or a Latvian residence permit, the days spent in Latvia under that permit do **not** count towards your 90-day visa-free allowance. 
</FAQ>
