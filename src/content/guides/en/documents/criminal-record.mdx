<QuickAnswer>
Almost all third-country nationals applying for a first-time residence permit must submit a police clearance certificate (statement on punishability). It must be issued within the last 6 months and usually requires an Apostille.
</QuickAnswer>

## Who needs to submit it?

If you are a non-EU citizen applying for a Latvian Temporary Residence Permit (for work, study, family, etc.) for the first time, you must submit a police clearance certificate.

* **Exemptions:** Children under the age of 14 do not need to provide a police clearance.

## Which country should issue it?

You must provide a certificate from:
1. Your country of citizenship.
2. **AND** any other country where you have lived for more than 12 months in the past.

*Example: If you are an Indian citizen but you just finished a 2-year master's degree in the UK, you must provide a police clearance from India AND a police clearance from the UK.*

## Validity and Requirements

PMLP has strict rules for this document:
* **Validity:** The certificate must be issued **no more than 6 months** before the day you submit your application to PMLP or the embassy.
* **Apostille:** Unless the document was issued by an EU/EEA country (or a country with a specific bilateral agreement with Latvia), the certificate **must** have an [Apostille or be legalised](./apostille-legalisation).
* **Translation:** If the certificate is not in English, Latvian, Russian, French, or German, it must be officially [translated](./certified-translations).

<WarningBox>
Getting a police clearance and an Apostille can take weeks or even months in some countries. Start this process as early as possible. If the document is older than 6 months when you apply, PMLP will reject it.
</WarningBox>

## Getting a Latvian Police Clearance
If you already live in Latvia and need a Latvian criminal record check (for example, to apply for a visa to another country, or for a specific job in Latvia), you can request it online via the Latvija.lv portal. The Information Centre of the Ministry of the Interior will issue it, and you can pay a small fee to have it officially stamped and mailed to you.
