<QuickAnswer>
Turkish citizens need a visa to enter Latvia. Applications are generally handled by the Latvian Embassy in Ankara. Documents issued in Turkey must be Apostilled by Turkish authorities.
</QuickAnswer>

## Visas and Embassy

Turkey is not a visa-free country for Latvia. If you are a Turkish citizen (holding a standard passport), you must apply for a Schengen Visa (Type C) for short visits, or a National Visa (Type D) / Residence permit for long stays.

* **Latvian Embassy in Turkey:** The primary diplomatic mission handling applications is the Embassy of the Republic of Latvia in Ankara. 
* **Visa Centers:** In many cases, standard Schengen visa applications are submitted through an external service provider (like VFS Global) in major Turkish cities, not directly at the embassy. Check the embassy's website for the current exact procedure.

## Document Legalisation (Apostille)

Turkey is a member of the Hague Apostille Convention. This is great news, because it means **you do not need full legalisation** for Turkish documents to be used in Latvia.

You only need an **Apostille**.

* **Where to get it:** In Turkey, Apostilles are typically issued by the local Governorates (Valilik) or District Governorates (Kaymakamlık). 
* **Police Clearance:** Your Turkish criminal record check (Adli Sicil Kaydı) must be Apostilled by the Turkish authorities before you translate it for PMLP.
* **Birth/Marriage Certificates:** "Formul A" (Birth) and "Formul B" (Marriage) are multilingual extracts. Check with the embassy if these specific multilingual forms still require an Apostille, as international agreements sometimes waive the Apostille requirement for these exact forms. Otherwise, standard Turkish certificates definitely need an Apostille.

## Translation
Because documents must be in English, Latvian, Russian, French, or German, your Turkish documents must be translated. You can use a sworn translator in Turkey, but remember that the translator's signature must be notarized, and the notary's stamp might also need an Apostille. 

To avoid complications, many people get the Turkish Apostille first, and then have the document translated by a certified translation agency in Latvia.
