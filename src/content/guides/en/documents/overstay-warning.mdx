<QuickAnswer>
Never overstay your visa or permit. Remaining in Latvia illegally can result in detention, deportation, heavy fines, and a multi-year entry ban covering the entire Schengen area.
</QuickAnswer>

## What is an Overstay?

You are overstaying if:
* Your 90-day visa-free allowance has run out.
* Your Schengen C-Visa or National D-Visa expires, and you are still in Latvia.
* Your Residence Permit expires, and you have not submitted a valid application for a new one.

<WarningBox>
**Applying for a permit does not automatically extend your visa.** If you are in Latvia on a 90-day visa and you submit a residence permit application to PMLP, you must still leave the country before day 90 if the permit is not yet approved. Alternatively, you must apply for a bridging D-Visa if eligible.
</WarningBox>

## The Consequences

Latvia enforces Schengen border rules strictly. If you are caught overstaying (usually when trying to fly out of the Riga airport, or during a random police check):

1. **Fines:** You will likely be fined a significant amount of money at the border.
2. **Voluntary Return Decision:** You will be issued a formal decision ordering you to leave the country within a specific number of days.
3. **Deportation:** If you do not leave voluntarily, or if you are deemed a flight risk, you can be detained in an accommodation center for detained foreigners and forcefully deported.
4. **Entry Ban:** You will receive an entry ban (usually 1 to 3 years, sometimes longer) recorded in the Schengen Information System (SIS). **This means you are banned from entering any of the 27 Schengen countries, not just Latvia.**

## What to do if you realize you have overstayed

Do not hide. The longer you overstay, the worse the penalty.
1. **Contact a Lawyer:** Speak to a licensed Latvian immigration lawyer immediately.
2. **Contact PMLP/Border Guard:** You (or your lawyer) should contact the State Border Guard or PMLP to arrange a "Voluntary Return." Showing a willingness to cooperate and leave the country at your own expense can sometimes result in a lighter penalty or a shorter entry ban.
