<QuickAnswer>
If you plan to walk in the forests or parks during the summer, you absolutely must get vaccinated against Tick-Borne Encephalitis. It requires a series of three shots.
</QuickAnswer>

## The Tick Threat (Ērces)

Latvia has a lot of beautiful, wild nature. Unfortunately, the forests and tall grass are heavily populated with ticks from April to October. 
Ticks in the Baltics carry two dangerous diseases:
1. **Lyme Disease (Laima slimība):** There is no vaccine for this. It is treated with antibiotics if caught early (look for the red bullseye rash).
2. **Tick-Borne Encephalitis (Ērču encefalīts):** A severe viral infection that attacks the brain and nervous system. It can cause permanent paralysis or death. There is no cure, but **there is a highly effective vaccine.**

<WarningBox>
**Get the Encephalitis Vaccine.** Almost every local Latvian is vaccinated against this. Do not skip it if you plan to pick mushrooms, hike in Gauja National Park, or even just sit on the grass in Mežaparks.
</WarningBox>

### How to get the Vaccine
You do not need a doctor's referral. 
You can walk into any major private clinic (ARS, VC4) or dedicated vaccination center, pay around **35 EUR**, and get the shot immediately. 
* **The Schedule:** You need three doses. Shot 1, then Shot 2 (1-3 months later), then Shot 3 (9-12 months later). You will need a booster every 3 to 5 years after that.

## Routine Childhood Vaccinations
If you are moving to Latvia with children, the state provides a free National Immunization Schedule (covering Polio, Measles, Mumps, Rubella, Diphtheria, etc.). You arrange these through your Family Doctor.

## Flu and Covid-19
Annual flu shots and Covid-19 boosters are readily available every autumn at pharmacies and clinics. They are usually free for high-risk groups (seniors, pregnant women) and cost a small fee (around 15-20 EUR) for everyone else.
