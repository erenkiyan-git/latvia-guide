<QuickAnswer>
Latvian is phonetic; you pronounce every letter exactly as it is written. The lines above vowels make them long, and the little hooks under consonants make them soft.
</QuickAnswer>

## 1. The Macrons (The Long Marks)
If a vowel has a straight line over it (a macron or *garumzīme*), you just hold the sound twice as long.
* **a** (like in "cut") vs **ā** (like the 'a' in "father")
* **e** (like in "bed") vs **ē** (like the 'ea' in "bear")
* **i** (like in "sit") vs **ī** (like the 'ee' in "see")
* **u** (like in "put") vs **ū** (like the 'oo' in "moon")

*Example:* "Rīga" is pronounced Ree-ga, not Rih-ga.

## 2. The Soft Marks (The Hooks)
If a consonant has a little hook under it (*mīkstinājuma zīme*), it softens the sound, almost as if you are adding a tiny 'y' after it.
* **ļ** – sounds like the 'lli' in million.
* **ņ** – sounds like the 'ni' in onion (or the Spanish 'ñ').
* **ķ** – sounds like 'ky' (a very soft K).
* **ģ** – sounds like 'gy' (a very soft G).

## 3. The Tricky Consonants
A few letters look like English but sound completely different:
* **c** – Always pronounced like the 'ts' in "cats". (Never like a 'k' or 's').
* **č** – Pronounced like the 'ch' in "church".
* **j** – Always pronounced like the 'y' in "yes". (Never like 'j' in "jump").
* **š** – Pronounced like the 'sh' in "shoe".
* **ž** – Pronounced like the 's' in "measure" or the French "Jacques".

*Example Name Check:* The street "Čaka iela" is pronounced "Chaka ee-ela". The city "Jelgava" is pronounced "Yel-ga-va".
