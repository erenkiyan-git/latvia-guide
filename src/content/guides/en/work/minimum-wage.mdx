<QuickAnswer>
Latvia has a national minimum wage that applies to everyone. However, if you are a non-EU citizen applying for a Work Residence Permit, your employer must pay you significantly more—usually equal to the national average gross wage.
</QuickAnswer>

## The National Minimum Wage
The Latvian government sets a national monthly minimum gross wage (Minimālā mēneša darba alga) that changes almost every year. No full-time employee in Latvia can legally be paid less than this amount. 

* **Where to check:** The Ministry of Welfare (Labklājības ministrija) publishes the current exact rate.
* **Who it applies to:** EU citizens, permanent residents, students working part-time, and locals.

## The Threshold for Non-EU Foreign Workers
If you are a third-country national applying for a standard **Work Residence Permit**, the national minimum wage does not apply to you. You are subject to a much higher threshold.

The government requires foreign workers to be paid **at least the average gross monthly wage of workers in Latvia in the preceding year**, as calculated by the Central Statistical Bureau (CSB).

*Why?* The law is designed to ensure companies only hire foreigners for skilled positions when they cannot find local talent, rather than importing cheap labor. 

<WarningBox>
**This threshold updates every April.** Every year on April 1st, PMLP updates the required minimum salary for foreign workers based on the new statistics. Your employment contract must state this gross amount or higher.
</WarningBox>

## The EU Blue Card Threshold
If you are applying for the highly-skilled EU Blue Card, the salary requirement is even higher. Your salary must usually be at least **1.2 or 1.5 times the national average gross wage**, depending on your specific profession.
