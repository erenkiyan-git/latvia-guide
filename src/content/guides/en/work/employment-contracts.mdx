<QuickAnswer>
An employment contract in Latvia must be in writing. It must state your gross salary, working hours, and job location. Never start working without signing a written contract first.
</QuickAnswer>

## The Legal Requirements

Latvian labor law is very strict and generally protects the employee. According to the Labour Law (Darba likums), every employment contract (Darba līgums) **must** be concluded in writing before you start work.

If you are a foreigner who does not speak Latvian, the employer should provide the contract in two columns (Latvian and English). However, in a legal dispute, the Latvian text takes precedence.

### Essential Elements of the Contract

Your contract must clearly state:
1. **The Parties:** Your name, personal code (personas kods), and address; and the company's registration number and legal address.
2. **Start Date:** The exact date your employment begins.
3. **Duration:** Whether the contract is for an indefinite period (standard) or a fixed term (e.g., covering maternity leave).
4. **Workplace Location:** The specific address where you will work (or stating that it is a remote role).
5. **Job Title and Profession Code:** Every job in Latvia has an official state classification code.
6. **Salary (Darba samaksa):** This must be stated as a **Gross (Bruto)** amount per month or per hour. It cannot be lower than the national minimum wage.
7. **Payday:** The exact date(s) of the month when your salary will be transferred to your bank account.
8. **Working Hours:** Usually 40 hours per week for full-time.
9. **Annual Leave:** The standard is 4 calendar weeks per year.

<WarningBox>
**The "Envelope Wage" Warning:** Never agree to a contract that states the minimum wage on paper while the employer promises to pay the rest of your salary in cash (aplokšņu alga). This is illegal tax evasion. It ruins your pension, your sick pay, and your ability to get a bank loan or renew your residence permit.
</WarningBox>

## Probation Period (Pārbaudes laiks)
Most contracts include a probation period. By law, it cannot exceed **3 months**. During this time, both you and the employer can terminate the contract with just 3 days' written notice, without needing to provide a reason.
