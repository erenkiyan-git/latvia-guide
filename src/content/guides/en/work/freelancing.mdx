<QuickAnswer>
If you want to work as a freelancer, Wolt courier, or independent contractor, you must register as a self-employed person (Saimnieciskās darbības veicējs) with the State Revenue Service (VID) and pay your own taxes.
</QuickAnswer>

## Who needs to register?
Anyone who regularly provides services for money independently, rather than under an employment contract. This includes:
* IT freelancers and designers.
* Food delivery couriers (Wolt, Bolt Food).
* Ride-hailing drivers (Bolt, Yandex).
* Private tutors.

*Note: Non-EU citizens must check their visa conditions. A standard Work Residence Permit is tied to a specific employer and does not allow freelance work. Students can freelance, but must stick to the 20-hour limit.*

## How to Register
The process is entirely digital.
1. Log into the **VID EDS** portal using your e-Signature or Smart-ID.
2. Fill out the application to register economic activity (Reģistrēt saimniecisko darbību).
3. You must select your primary NACE code (the official code representing the type of work you do, e.g., "Computer programming" or "Postal and courier activities").

## Tax Regimes for Freelancers
Latvia offers a few different tax regimes for self-employed people. You must choose one when you register.

### 1. General Tax Regime
You pay standard Personal Income Tax (IIN) on your profit (Income minus business expenses). You must also pay Social Contributions (VSAOI) if your income exceeds a certain monthly threshold. You must keep detailed records of all your receipts and expenses.

### 2. Micro-Enterprise Tax (MUN)
This is popular for freelancers with very low expenses (like IT consultants or designers). 
* You pay a flat percentage tax (usually 25%) on your **total turnover** (all the money you receive), regardless of your expenses. 
* You cannot deduct business expenses. 
* This single payment covers both your income tax and social tax. 
* The turnover limit for this regime is capped annually.

## Invoicing
When you work as a freelancer, you must issue legal invoices (Rēķins) to your clients. The invoice must contain your name, personal code, declared address, bank details, and the details of the service provided.
