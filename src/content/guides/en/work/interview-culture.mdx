<QuickAnswer>
Latvian workplace culture is a mix of Northern European efficiency and Eastern European formality. Interviews are direct and fact-based. Punctuality is non-negotiable.
</QuickAnswer>

## The Interview Process

* **Format:** For corporate jobs, expect 2 to 3 rounds of interviews. The first is usually a short HR screening call, followed by an interview with the hiring manager, and often a practical test or case study.
* **Punctuality:** Arrive exactly on time (5 minutes early). Being late to an interview without calling ahead is almost an automatic rejection.
* **Dress Code:** "Business casual" is the safest bet for interviews. Full suits are usually only required for banking and law firms. Tech companies are very casual, but it is better to be slightly overdressed for the interview.
* **Directness:** Interviewers will ask direct questions about your skills and experience. They will not spend 10 minutes making small talk about the weather. Be prepared to give concrete examples of your past work.

## Workplace Culture

Once you get the job, here is what you should expect in a typical Latvian office:

### 1. Work-Life Balance
Latvia has strong labor laws. The standard workweek is 40 hours (Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 18:00). Staying late is generally not expected or praised in modern companies, though startups might be an exception. People value their weekends and time spent in nature.

### 2. Formality and "Jūs" vs "Tu"
The Latvian language has formal ("Jūs") and informal ("Tu") ways of saying "You". 
In a traditional corporate setting, you use the formal "Jūs" with bosses and older colleagues until they invite you to use "Tu". In international IT companies speaking English, this hierarchy is flattened, and everyone uses first names.

### 3. Name Days (Vārda diena)
This is a massive cultural tradition. Every day of the calendar year is assigned to specific Latvian names. When it is a colleague's Name Day, it is treated almost like a second birthday. The person celebrating usually brings snacks, chocolates, or a cake to the office kitchen for everyone to share. 

### 4. Communication Style
Latvians are reserved. They do not typically smile at strangers or engage in loud, enthusiastic greetings. Silence during a meeting is normal and means people are thinking, not that they are angry. Once you break the ice and build trust, Latvians are incredibly loyal and warm colleagues.
