<QuickAnswer>
Never pay an agency to "find" you a job in Latvia, and never pay an employer for a work contract. Legitimate recruitment is free for the job seeker. 
</QuickAnswer>

## 1. The "Pay for a Job" Scam
This is the most common scam targeting workers from Asia and Africa. A "recruitment agency" contacts you online, offering a guaranteed job in a Latvian factory, farm, or construction site. They ask you to pay a large upfront fee (often 1000 - 3000 EUR) for "visa processing," "document translation," or "securing the contract."
* **The Reality:** Once you pay, they either disappear, or they send you a fake PMLP invitation number that the embassy rejects. 
* **The Rule:** In Latvia, the employer pays the recruitment agency, not the employee. It is illegal to charge a job seeker a fee for placement.

## 2. The "Fake Work Contract" for a Visa
Some fraudulent companies exist solely to sell fake employment contracts to foreigners who just want a visa to enter the Schengen area.
* **The Reality:** PMLP actively investigates suspicious companies. If they discover you bought a fake contract and never actually worked there, your residence permit will be immediately annulled, you will be deported, and you will receive a Schengen entry ban for immigration fraud.

## 3. The "Envelope Wage" (Aplokšņu alga)
An employer offers you a job and says: "I will write the minimum wage in your official contract so we pay fewer taxes, but I will give you the rest of your salary in an envelope in cash every month."
* **The Reality:** This is illegal tax evasion. It is a massive risk for you. 
* You will not be able to renew your residence permit because your official income won't meet the PMLP threshold.
* The employer can simply refuse to pay the cash portion, and you cannot complain to the Labour Inspectorate because it isn't in your contract.

## How to Verify an Employer
Before accepting a job or sending your passport details, look up the company on the official Latvian Enterprise Register database (**Lursoft** or **Firmas.lv**).
* Check if the company actually exists.
* Check if they have tax debts (VID administrētie nodokļu parādi). A company with tax debts cannot legally sponsor a foreign worker.

<WarningBox>
If an offer sounds too good to be true, requires upfront cash, or bypasses official channels, walk away. You can report fraudulent employers to the State Labour Inspectorate (VDI) or the State Police.
</WarningBox>
