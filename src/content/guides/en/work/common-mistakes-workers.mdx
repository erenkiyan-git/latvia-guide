<QuickAnswer>
The most common mistakes are accepting "envelope wages" which ruins your safety net, forgetting to submit your electronic tax booklet, and assuming you can freelance on a standard work permit.
</QuickAnswer>

## 1. Accepting Envelope Wages (Aplokšņu alga)
This is the biggest mistake. A boss might offer to officially pay you the minimum wage and give you the rest in cash to "save on taxes."
* **The Consequence:** You will not be able to renew your Work Residence Permit because your official income will not meet the PMLP threshold. Furthermore, if you get sick or lose your job, your state benefits will be calculated based only on the minimum wage, leaving you with almost nothing.

## 2. Forgetting the Tax Booklet (Algas nodokļa grāmatiņa)
You must log into the VID EDS system and electronically assign your tax booklet to your new employer.
* **The Consequence:** If you do not do this, your employer is legally forced to tax your entire salary at the highest possible rate, meaning your monthly Net take-home pay will be significantly lower than it should be.

## 3. Quitting Before Securing a New Sponsor
If you hate your job and want to resign, remember that your standard Work Residence Permit is tied directly to that specific employer.
* **The Consequence:** The moment you resign, your residence permit is annulled. You have 30 days to leave the country. You must secure a new job offer and wait for the new employer to post the vacancy on the NVA portal *before* you resign from your current job.

## 4. Freelancing on a Tied Permit
Many expats think they can drive for Bolt or do graphic design on the side to earn extra cash.
* **The Consequence:** A standard Work Residence Permit explicitly restricts you to working ONLY for the employer that sponsored you. If you register as a self-employed person (freelancer) with the state revenue service, PMLP will see it, and it can jeopardize your immigration status. 

## 5. Ignoring Office Etiquette
Latvian work culture is highly punctual and direct. 
* **The Consequence:** Arriving 10 minutes late to meetings, ignoring the formal "Jūs" (You) when speaking to senior Latvian management, or expecting constant enthusiastic praise for doing basic tasks will quickly isolate you from your colleagues.
