<QuickAnswer>
The 10.5% deducted from your salary for VSAOI is essentially state insurance. It guarantees you paid sick leave, maternity/paternity pay, unemployment benefits, and a future pension.
</QuickAnswer>

## What is VSAOI?

VSAOI stands for *Valsts sociālās apdrošināšanas obligātās iemaksas* (State Social Insurance Mandatory Contributions). 

Every time you get paid, a portion of your gross salary goes directly to the State Social Insurance Agency (VSAA). This is the most important tax you pay because it directly funds your personal safety net.

## What does it cover?

By paying VSAOI, you are legally entitled to:
* **Sickness Benefits:** If you get sick and the doctor issues an electronic [Sick Leave B](../work/probation-leave) certificate, the state pays you 80% of your average salary for the duration of the illness.
* **Maternity and Paternity Benefits:** Paid leave when you have a child.
* **Parental Benefits:** Paid leave (usually up to 1.5 years) to stay home and care for a newborn.
* **Unemployment Benefits:** If you lose your job (and have paid VSAOI for at least 12 months in the last 16 months), the state will pay you a monthly benefit while you look for a new job.
* **State Pension:** You are building your retirement fund.

## Why "Envelope Wages" are dangerous
If an employer pays you in cash and does not officially declare your full salary to the state, they are stealing your VSAOI. 

If you get sick, the state will only pay you 80% of your *official* minimum wage salary, not your actual cash earnings. If you lose your job, your unemployment benefits will be virtually zero.
