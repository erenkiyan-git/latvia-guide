<QuickAnswer>
University dormitories are the cheapest option (100–250 EUR/month) and provide an instant community, but you will share a room and facilities. Private renting offers privacy but is much more expensive and harder to secure from abroad.
</QuickAnswer>

## University Dormitories (Kopmītnes / Dienesta viesnīca)

Almost all major universities in Latvia own student hostels or dormitories.

**Pros:**
* **Cost:** Very affordable. Prices typically range from 100 EUR to 250 EUR per month, and utilities (heating, water, electricity, internet) are usually included in the price.
* **Easy to book:** You can book a room directly through your university before you arrive in Latvia.
* **Visa/Permit Proof:** The dormitory will provide you with an official letter of accommodation, which perfectly satisfies the PMLP requirement for your residence permit.
* **Community:** The fastest way to meet other international and local students.

**Cons:**
* **Privacy:** You will almost certainly share a room with 1 or 2 other students. Single rooms are rare and highly sought after.
* **Facilities:** Bathrooms and kitchens are usually shared by the whole floor or a block of rooms.
* **Rules:** Dorms have strict rules (e.g., quiet hours, guest registration, no smoking).

## Private Student Hostels
There are private companies (like SHED Co-living or Duck Republik in Riga) that offer modern, purpose-built student housing. These are more expensive than university dorms (often 300–500 EUR/month) but offer better facilities, private bathrooms, gyms, and modern study areas.

## Private Renting

Renting a private apartment (or sharing an apartment with friends) is the standard choice for older students or those who want privacy.

**Pros:**
* Total privacy and independence.
* You choose the location (closer to the city center or your specific faculty).
* You get your own kitchen and bathroom.

**Cons:**
* **Cost:** Much more expensive. Renting a 1-bedroom apartment in Riga can cost 350–500+ EUR/month, plus you must pay [utilities separately](../housing/where-to-search) (which can be very high in winter).
* **Deposits:** You will need to pay 1 or 2 months' rent upfront as a security deposit.
* **Scams:** It is risky to rent an apartment remotely before you arrive. You should always view an apartment in person before signing or paying anything.
* **Declaring Address:** You must ensure the landlord officially allows you to [declare your address](../documents/address-declaration) there for your residence permit.

<FAQ title="Can I live in a dorm first, then move to an apartment?">
Yes, this is the most common path. Many international students book a university dorm for their first semester. This gives them time to arrive, get their residence permit, make friends, and then search for a private apartment to rent together.
</FAQ>
