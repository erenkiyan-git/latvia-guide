<QuickAnswer>
Living on a budget in Latvia means prioritizing university dormitories, cooking at home with ingredients from the Central Market or Lidl, and utilizing the massive public transport discounts for students.
</QuickAnswer>

## 1. Accommodation

Rent will be your biggest expense.
* **Cheapest:** University dormitories (100 - 250 EUR/month). 
* **If renting private:** Do not live in the Old Town (Vecrīga) or the Quiet Centre (Klusais centrs). Look at neighbourhoods further out but connected by tram or train (e.g., Āgenskalns, Teika, Jugla, Imanta).
* **Share:** Renting a larger 3-bedroom apartment and splitting it with two other students is significantly cheaper per person than renting a 1-bedroom apartment alone.

## 2. Food and Groceries

Eating at restaurants every day will drain your budget fast.
* **Supermarkets:** Lidl is generally the cheapest supermarket for basic goods. Maxima (especially Maxima XX and XXX) also has excellent weekly discounts. Rimi tends to be slightly more expensive, but their loyalty card offers good personal discounts.
* **Riga Central Market (Centrāltirgus):** The best place to buy fresh seasonal vegetables, fruits, meat, and dairy. It is often much cheaper than supermarkets, especially in the summer.
* **University Canteens:** Most universities have subsidized cafeterias where you can get a hot lunch for 4 - 6 EUR.

## 3. Transport

Do not use taxis (Bolt/Uber) daily.
* **Public Transport:** If you study in Riga, get the personalized e-ticket (Rīdzinieka karte/Studentu e-talons). Full-time students receive a massive discount on monthly tickets for buses, trams, and trolleybuses.
* **Walking/Cycling:** Riga is very flat and increasingly bike-friendly. Buying a cheap second-hand bicycle is a great investment for the warmer months.

## 4. Student Discounts

Always carry your student ID!
* **ISIC Card:** The [ISIC card](./isic-card) gives you discounts on intercity trains, buses, cinemas, museums, and many cafes.
* **Free Culture:** Most national museums are free or heavily discounted for students on specific days of the month.
* **Libraries:** The National Library of Latvia (LNB) is an incredible, free, warm place to study with fast internet.

<FAQ title="Can I survive on 400 EUR a month?">
It is extremely difficult. 400 EUR might just cover a cheap dorm room and basic groceries, leaving nothing for transport, study materials, clothes, or emergencies. A more realistic minimum budget for a student in Riga is 600 - 800 EUR per month.
</FAQ>
