<QuickAnswer>
Erasmus students usually stay for 1 or 2 semesters. If you are an EU citizen, the process is incredibly easy. If you are a non-EU citizen studying at an EU university, you must apply for a Latvian Long-Stay Visa (Type D) for your exchange period.
</QuickAnswer>

## The EU vs Non-EU Difference

The Erasmus+ program facilitates the academic exchange, but it does not bypass national immigration laws.

### For EU/EEA Citizens
If you hold a passport from an EU country:
* You do not need a visa.
* You can simply buy a plane ticket, arrive in Riga, and start studying.
* If your exchange lasts longer than 90 days, you must [register your stay](../documents/eu-eea-registration) at PMLP and declare your address. That's it.

### For Non-EU Citizens (studying in the EU)
If you are, for example, a Turkish, Indian, or Brazilian citizen currently studying at a university in Germany or Poland, and you want to do an Erasmus semester in Latvia:
* **You cannot just arrive and stay for 6 months on your German/Polish residence permit.**
* You must apply for a **Latvian National D-Visa** before you arrive, or immediately upon arrival (if you have visa-free entry rights to the Schengen area).
* The D-Visa for exchange students requires an invitation from the Latvian host university, proof of your Erasmus grant, and your passport.

<WarningBox>
**Do not ignore the visa rule.** Many non-EU students mistakenly believe their Italian or Spanish student residence permit allows them to live in Latvia for a 6-month Erasmus exchange. It does not. It only allows visits of up to 90 days. You must get the Latvian D-Visa.
</WarningBox>

## Getting Involved (ESN)

The Erasmus Student Network (ESN) is incredibly active in Riga.
* **ESN Riga:** They organize welcome weeks, parties, city tours, and trips to neighboring countries (Estonia, Lithuania, Finland).
* **Buddy System:** Most Latvian universities offer a "Buddy" program where a local student helps you navigate your first weeks (picking you up from the airport, showing you where the supermarkets are). Sign up for this!

## Accommodation

Erasmus students usually choose between:
1. **University Dorms:** Cheap, but heavily oversubscribed. Apply the exact minute the application opens.
2. **Erasmus Hostels/Co-living:** Places like Duck Republik cater heavily to the Erasmus crowd. They are expensive but guarantee an instant social life.
3. **Flatshares:** Many local landlords rent large apartments in the city center room-by-room to Erasmus students. Be cautious of scams; never pay a deposit before seeing the flat or verifying the landlord.
